The Native Soil Project: 2026/27 Pilot Study

The Native Soil Project is now underway with its first-year pilot study. Two partner sites less than an hour apart, one hosted by Tom Forrest in the Yarra Valley and the other by Dr Mary Cole in Cardinia Shire, are growing the same industrial hemp clones under a shared program that tightly manages variables, so each site grows its plants as similarly as possible. The pilot asks a simple question: how much do small differences in microclimate and soil type shape the flowers a plant produces? This is the terroir effect. Through close site-to-site observation across the season, and laboratory analysis of flower chemistry at harvest, the study will record how place shows up in the crop. The pilot is also a test of the method itself: where it can be improved, what it will take to fund, and how to grow it into a larger study with more partner sites and genetics, building towards a regional map and, in time, an appellation framework. Follow the study's progress here and on the Grounded Cultivation Instagram.

Research papers

The research that informs the Native Soil Project, from cannabis and hemp to the wine regions that pioneered terroir science.

Cannabis and terroir

The terroir of cannabis: terpene metabolomics as a tool to understand Cannabis sativa selections

Mudge, Brown & Murch (2019)

The paper that introduced terroir to the cannabis literature, comparing terpene profiles across producers. It couldn't separate place from genetics, which is the gap a same-clone design addresses.

Comparison of the cannabinoid and terpene profiles in commercial cannabis from natural and artificial cultivation

Zandkarimi, Nuckolls et al. (2023)

The same clones grown outdoors in living soil and indoors, showing richer terpene profiles from the outdoor plants.

Impact of soil quality on cannabinoid and terpenoid content of Cannabis sativa L.

Chacon, Raup-Konsavage et al. (2025)

Penn State and USDA research comparing cover-crop no-till with conventional tillage across two hemp cultivars. Soil management shifted the plants' chemistry, in different directions for each cultivar.

Toward a cannabis terroir: untargeted metabolomic profiling of authentic samples

Poetzsch, Steuer et al. (2025)

Uses the plant's full chemical fingerprint to tell growing environments apart, setting a benchmark for how terroir can be measured.

Influence of altitude on phytochemical composition of hemp inflorescence

Giupponi, Leoni, Pavlovic & Giorgi (2020)

The same hemp variety grown at 1,200 m and at 130 m, with altitude measurably changing the plants' chemistry.

Phytochemical and ecological analysis of two varieties of hemp grown in a mountain environment of the Italian Alps

Pavlovic, Panseri, Giupponi et al. (2019)

A companion study from the same group, adding ecological context for hemp grown at altitude.

Understanding cultivar-specificity and soil determinants of the cannabis microbiome

Winston et al. (2014)

Shows that soil type mainly shapes the microbial community around the roots, while the cultivar mainly shapes the microbes inside them.

Potential impacts of soil microbiota manipulation on secondary metabolites production in cannabis

Ahmed & Hijri (2021)

A review of how soil microbial communities could be managed to influence the plant's chemistry.

UV-B radiation effects on photosynthesis, growth and cannabinoid production of two Cannabis sativa chemotypes

Lydon, Teramura & Coffman (1987)

A classic study on how ultraviolet light, which varies with altitude and latitude, affects growth and plant chemistry.

Wine and terroir

Preliminary study of Australian Pinot Noir wines by colour and volatile analyses

Foods (2020)

Compares Pinot Noir from the Yarra Valley, Mornington Peninsula, Adelaide Hills and Tasmania, showing that regional wines can be told apart by their aroma compounds. A model for regional terpene work.

Pinot Noir provenance: Australian benchmarking

Wine Australia

The research project behind regional provenance testing for Australian Pinot Noir.

The value of soil knowledge in understanding wine terroir

Robert White, University of Melbourne (2020)

Reviews how soil water, nitrogen, temperature and microbes shape terroir, and argues for detailed soil mapping and long-term monitoring.

McLaren Vale: Geology of a Wine Region

Map and guide

Maps 55 geological formations across one wine region, a model for compiling regional soil and geology data.
