Eastern Highlands Cannabis

Granite Belt, QLD

@ehcaustralia on Instagram

High on Queensland's Granite Belt, Kaston Anderson and Victoria Pearson are proving that a licensed cultivation site doesn't need a big budget to grow healthy plants.

At a glance

Region
Granite Belt, Queensland
Elevation
About 900 m
Soil
Decomposed granite
Growing
Native soil in raised beds, organically fed
Crop cycles
At least three a year

Who they are

Kaston and Victoria run Eastern Highlands with a small team and a lean budget, by design. Their aim is to show that a licensed farm can be built and run without the large capital investment the sector often assumes.

Where they grow

The Granite Belt sits high on the Great Dividing Range, in the coolest corner of Queensland. Granite formed around 200 million years ago has weathered into coarse, free-draining soils, the same ground that supports the region's wineries. Summers are warm, nights are cool and winters bring regular frost.

How they grow

Their raised beds are built from the farm's own soil and amended with compost to build organic matter and soil life. Six low-tech polytunnels keep the crop under the sun while sheltering it from frost, hail and heavy rain. Covering the tunnels to shorten the day lets the team set when plants flower, fitting at least three crop cycles into the year.

Their belief is simple: grown organically, in native soil and under the sun, plants respond fully to the place they're grown.
